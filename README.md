The dominant bottleneck on this i5-12500H is memory traffic, not arithmetic. A stride-1 scan of the 134 MB buffer costs 2.27 ns per double, while stride 8 costs 6.97 ns because each read takes its own 64-byte cache line. Interchanging the dense kernel from i-j-k to i-k-j makes that row stream contiguous and speeds the N=1536 block up by 20.2x. Putting eight counters on one cache line makes 8 processes slower than 1 (Karp-Flatt e(8)=3.04); padding or a private accumulator removes the collapse. The 5-point stencil does only 0.31 flops per DRAM byte, under the 3.58 flop/byte ridge of dual-channel DDR5-4800, and its 37.7 MB grid is larger than the 18.9 MB L3, so strong-scaling efficiency falls to 32% at 12 processes with an Amdahl ceiling of 6.55.
